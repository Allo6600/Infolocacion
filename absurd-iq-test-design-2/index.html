Bell Curve Scoring

Normal distribution (bell curve)

IQ scores are fitted to a symmetric bell-shaped curve, so most people cluster near the middle. Many are surprised that the shape is imposed by design. An absurd test can bell-curve anything, like yawn frequency.

Norm-referenced testing

A raw score means nothing until compared with a reference sample of people. The surprise is that changing the sample changes your score. Parody designers enjoy choosing wildly unfit norm groups, such as pigeons or octopuses.

Modern IQ is not a quotient at all: the average is simply set to 100 and the standard deviation to 15. People are surprised these numbers are conventions. Any silly measurement can be rescaled the same way.

Test-retest reliability

A test is reliable if the same person gets similar scores on repeated sittings. Surprisingly, reliability says nothing about whether the test measures anything meaningful. A nonsense test can be perfectly consistent, which designers happily exploit.
